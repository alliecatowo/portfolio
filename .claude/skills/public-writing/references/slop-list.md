# AI-slop tells

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" field guide plus common LLM habits. These signs do not prove a text is AI-written; humans share some. They are the habits that make Allison's writing sound generic whatever wrote the draft. `scripts/check-prose.mjs` enforces the phrase list; the structural items need your eye.

## Vocabulary and phrases (banned)

delve, tapestry, testament ("a testament to"), landscape ("the evolving landscape"), realm, navigate ("navigate the complexities"), leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionary, transformative, groundbreaking, paradigm shift, synergy, holistic, pivotal, crucial, vital, intricate, multifaceted, underscores, showcases, fosters, journey, deep dive, dive into.

Openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving", "In an era of", "Let's dive in", "Without further ado", "In conclusion", "In summary", "At the end of the day", "Moving forward", "It's no secret that", "Whether you're a ... or a ...".

Hedging and filler: "it's worth noting", "it is important to note", "needless to say", "essentially", "arguably", "truly", "incredibly", "I'd be remiss".

Voice tics: "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "Let me be clear", "Let that sink in", "Read that again", "Buckle up", "I'm excited to announce", "I'm thrilled", "I'm humbled".

## Structures (catch by eye)

- "Not just X, but Y", "It's not about X, it's about Y", "X isn't Y, it's Z", "More than just".
- A rhetorical question followed by its own answer, especially as an opener or section header.
- Rule of three: three adjectives, three parallel clauses, three bullets that say the same thing.
- Every paragraph ending on a quotable one-liner; a closing line that restates the whole post.
- Em dashes standing in for commas, colons and parentheses. Target at most 1-2 per 500 words.
- Staccato fragments for drama ("Fast. Simple. Done.").
- Fake vulnerability and fake stakes ("I almost quit", "Nobody talks about this") unless true and sourced.
- Vague attribution ("experts say", "studies show") with no name or link.
- Emoji bullets, an emoji on every header, bold on every list lead-in, Title Case Headings, "Key takeaways" boxes.
- Symmetric "pros and cons" or "challenges and opportunities" sections with nothing specific in them.
- A closing paragraph that repeats the intro.

## Fixes

- Replace the abstract noun with the thing: not "a robust solution" but "22 tools, each with a typed schema".
- Delete the sentence before the one that matters.
- Say the number. If there is no number, say what you saw.
- Use "I" and past tense for what you did.
